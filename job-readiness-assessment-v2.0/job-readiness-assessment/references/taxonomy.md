# Skills and competencies taxonomy

Load at workflow steps 4–6.

## 1. Skill layers

Link each skill to its ESCO concept where possible (`references/sources.md`), so skills can be compared across postings and fields.

| Layer | Content | How to select |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Universal** | Analytical thinking, communication, collaboration, digital literacy, AI literacy, business literacy, problem-solving, project execution, professional effectiveness, ethics, career management, languages and intercultural competence | Relevant to almost any role; pick those the posting needs |
| **B — Background** | Skills from the person's education, training or previous field, in any discipline (e.g. nursing, mechanical engineering, law, design, hospitality, finance) | From the profile; map to ESCO skills where possible |
| **C — Role** | Technical and domain skills the posting asks for, including tools, methods, licences and certifications | From the posting; corroborate with the ESCO occupation profile |
| **D — Durable human skills** | Personal, social, learning and transformative skills (Section 4) | From behavioural evidence only |

Where Layer A overlaps Layer D (communication, collaboration, critical thinking), record the evidence once under Layer D.

Tools and platforms are mandatory only if the posting says so.

## 2. Competencies

**Definition.** ESCO, following the European Qualifications Framework, defines competence as the proven ability to use knowledge, skills and personal, social and/or methodological abilities in work or study situations; a competence is broader than a skill: applying knowledge and skills independently in new situations [S17].

**Structure of a role competency**
- **Name and definition:** what the person does in the role, in one sentence.
- **Component skills:** 1–4 skills from Layers A–D.
- **Knowledge:** what must be understood (employer's business, rules of the sector).
- **Behaviours:** observable actions, linked to Layer D.
- **Context:** the work situation.

**How to build them.** Take the 3–6 main responsibilities of the posting; turn each into one competency; attach the requirements that serve it. A competency is "explicit" only if the posting names it; otherwise tag it "Inference" with the responsibility it came from.

**Example profiles** (proposals; adapt to the posting)

| Role | Competencies |
|---|---|
| Data / BI Analyst | Prepare trustworthy data · Analyse and interpret data · Communicate insight for decisions · Work with stakeholders · Use data and AI responsibly |
| Business Analyst | Understand the business problem · Gather and structure requirements · Analyse options with data · Communicate and align · Deliver change responsibly |
| Project / Programme Manager | Plan and scope · Manage risks and dependencies · Lead and coordinate people · Report to stakeholders · Deliver within budget |
| Sales / Account Manager | Find and qualify opportunities · Understand client needs · Negotiate and close · Manage accounts and pipeline |
| Software Engineer | Design solutions · Write and test maintainable code · Collaborate through code review · Operate and improve systems |
| Nurse (regulated) | Hold valid registration · Assess and plan care · Deliver safe care · Communicate with patients and teams · Document accurately |
| Team or department lead | Set direction · Develop people · Make decisions with data · Manage stakeholders and resources |

## 3. Explicit and implicit requirements

- **Explicit:** stated in the posting or the profile.
- **Implicit:** derived by a documented rule. Always tagged "Inference" with the rule and a confidence.

**Job side**

| Derivation source | Example | Derived | Max status | Max confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Responsibilities | "Present findings to the director" | Communication (D5) | Preferred | Medium |
| Deliverables | "Build monthly KPI reporting" | Metric definition, data quality | Preferred | Medium |
| Business model | Subscription company | Retention and churn metrics | Contextual | Medium |
| Sector rules | Bank, health provider, personal data | Regulatory and data-protection awareness | Contextual | Medium |
| Seniority wording | "Lead a team of five" | Leadership (D13), people development | Preferred | Medium |
| Organisation context | International team | Professional English, intercultural collaboration | Preferred | Medium |
| ESCO occupation profile | Essential skill missing from the posting | Candidate skill to check | Contextual | Low |

Implicit requirements are never "required". Keep only those that change the recommendations.

**Profile side**

| Derivation source | Example | Default label |
|---|---|---|
| Artefact content | Report with market study and recommendations | Demonstrated (if supplied) |
| Role context | Customer-service job handling complaints | Transferable |
| Previous field | Teacher moving into training design; nurse moving into health data | Transferable |
| Course or training title alone | "Introduction to SQL" | Needs verification |
| Volunteering, associations | Treasurer of a club | Transferable |

Implicit competencies cannot exceed L2 without a supporting artefact. Advise the person to make strong implicit competencies explicit in the CV, with evidence.

## 4. Durable human skills (Layer D)

Employers rank human-centred skills among the most important core skills: in the WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025, analytical thinking is first, followed by resilience, flexibility and agility, and leadership and social influence [S12]. In a WEF/Indeed assessment of over 2,800 granular skills, none showed very high capacity to be replaced by current generative AI [S12].

| ID | Skill | Source |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Self-regulation | LifeComp P1 [S15] |
| D2 | Flexibility | LifeComp P2 [S15] |
| D3 | Wellbeing | LifeComp P3 [S15] — **never assessed or stored** |
| D4 | Empathy | LifeComp S1 [S15] |
| D5 | Communication | LifeComp S2 [S15]; NACE [S16] |
| D6 | Collaboration | LifeComp S3 [S15]; NACE Teamwork [S16] |

| D8 | Critical thinking | LifeComp L2 [S15]; NACE [S16] |
| D9 | Managing learning | LifeComp L3 [S15] |
| D10 | Creating new value | OECD transformative competency [S13] |
| D11 | Reconciling tensions and dilemmas | OECD transformative competency [S13] |
| D12 | Taking responsibility | OECD transformative competency [S13]; NACE Professionalism [S16] |
| D13 | Leadership and social influence | WEF core skill [S12]; NACE Leadership [S16] |
| D15 | Responsible use of technology and AI | NACE Technology [S16] |

D14 (equity and inclusion) is excluded: NACE lists it as under review [S16].

**Evidence:** only behavioural examples (situation, action, result) from peer feedback, employer reviews, references, volunteering, assessed presentations. Personality labels and CV adjectives are not evidence. Where no example exists, mark "Not evidenced" and suggest how to gather one.
